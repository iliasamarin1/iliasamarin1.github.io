How students trust ChatGPT, and where that trust breaks

Students use ChatGPT to summarize readings and check facts. We wanted to know whether they notice when it gets something wrong. Mostly, they don't.

0 / 12noticed the key causes the summary left out
4 / 12opened the source article to check
33%found the built-in regenerate control
9 / 12rated the summary task easy

What I did

  • Ran a heuristic evaluation of ChatGPT against Nielsen's heuristics. I flagged icon-only controls and an unlabeled "Medium" effort setting.
  • Helped design and moderate 12 think-aloud sessions covering three tasks: summarize an article, resolve conflicting numbers, recover from a bad answer.
  • Synthesized the sessions with affinity clustering and rated each theme by frequency, impact and persistence.

What we found

  • Long, well-formatted summaries hide omissions. Readers skim the bold text and trust the structure.
  • ChatGPT sometimes accepted a wrong correction. Two participants accepted the wrong number along with it.
  • Recovery controls are hard to find, so most people retyped their prompt instead.

Problem statement we landed onHow might we help students notice when ChatGPT leaves out key information or changes a correct answer, without making it slower to use?

The tension that shaped it: 9 of 12 valued how fast the tool felt, so a fix that adds friction would get ignored. Our proposed direction is a lightweight "What might be missing?" check that sits next to the answer.

SneakySwing: the retention hypothesis we went in with was wrong

SneakySwing is an AI golf coach. You film your swing on your phone, and computer vision plus an LLM tell you what to fix. It had a live MVP and real users, but product decisions were running on assumptions.

11interviews over 5 weeks
7on-course sessions at Bob O'Connor Golf Course
3hypotheses tested: usability, trust, retention
6recommendations delivered to the founders
AreaWhat we assumedResultWhat users said
UsabilityUsers can navigate the app without major difficultyPartly confirmed"The interface is easy to navigate." But also: "I have been golfing for four years, and even I'm unsure what some of these things mean."
TrustStrong golfers will trust AI feedback lessConfirmed"It's training on someone else's swing. Someone taller is going to have a steeper swing. Will it know that?"
RetentionMid-to-advanced golfers will stay because the app gives them an extra edgeDisproven"Once you reach a certain point in golf, you are aware what parts of your swing need fixing. You can essentially coach yourself."

What we learned

  • The clean UI wasn't the problem. Playful, informal feedback language was, even for experienced players.
  • Every interviewee raised ball flight on their own. The app analyzes the swing, not where the ball goes.
  • Willingness to pay among mid-to-advanced golfers was zero. They already had coaches, or didn't value the feedback at their level. The better bet is beginners, where fast feedback matters most.

What we recommended

  1. Fix video capture. On the course, the app cut recordings off before the swing ended.
  2. Save every analyzed swing to the journal automatically.
  3. Draw skeleton overlays and club path on the video instead of describing them in text.
  4. Separate measured data from AI interpretation, so users know how much weight to give each.
  5. Let users tag shot outcomes, and say plainly that ball tracking isn't there yet.
  6. Calibrate for height, experience and swing style at onboarding.

The redesign

We turned each recommendation into a screen so the founders could react to something concrete.

Sample caveat we flagged: most interviewees were tech-comfortable students, and we couldn't track anyone past their first session.

03 / Discovery plan
Context
Guild PM internship take-home, presented in a project review with two Guild PMs
When
May 2026
Prompt
A bank partner wants an ROI report on its education benefit. How would you run the 45-minute discovery call?

An ROI request is a decision in disguise

"ROI" without a decision behind it is just a number looking for a meaning. A bank could mean branch turnover, getting tellers into licensed roles, promotion rates, or the workforce story it tells its board. So I planned the call around finding the decision first.

Goal for the 45 minutesWalk away with 1–3 talent decisions this report should inform. For each one: the evidence that would move it, who has to believe it, and what would make them distrust it.

BucketA question I'd askWhy it earns the time
Goals"What are the 1–2 talent priorities your head of HR is on the hook for in 2026?"Ties ROI to goals they're already measured on
ROI today"If you put one number in a board deck about this benefit, what would it be?"Shows what shape the answer needs to take
Stakeholders"Who else has to believe this report, and what will they object to?"Champions get the meeting; skeptics decide the renewal
Data"Have you tried to measure this yourselves? Where did it break?"Shows where we add value instead of rebuilding what they have
Definition of good"If we deliver exactly this, what do you do differently in the next 90 days?"If they can't answer, the report is decoration

What I planned beyond the call

Talk to the account team and data engineers before promising anything. Compare how other bank partners measure ROI, and hear from employees who use the benefit. Then put two deliberately different prototypes in front of the partner, one built for Finance and one for HR, and see which they react to.

How I used AI

I had Claude gather what's public about Guild's products and partners, then role-play a skeptical bank HR partner. Any question I couldn't defend in one line got cut. Deciding which questions deserved the 45 minutes was my call.

Typing on a one-inch screen

The challenge: let someone type full sentences on a smartwatch-sized square, quickly and without errors. Below is the final design, rebuilt so you can try it. Tap keys or the blue suggestion bar.

The timer starts on your first tap. WPM counts five characters as a word, the same way the class bakeoff scored it.

What changed between rounds

  • Round 1: a mini QWERTY keyboard with a spelling-correction bar and an enlarged preview of the key under your finger.
  • Round 2: a navy theme with color-coded keys (red ⌫, blue space bar). The suggestion bar now shows your typed prefix next to the completion.
  • Round 3: users kept mis-hitting delete and space, so I rebuilt those targets. I also cut the OK button: it added a tap and caught nothing. And I replaced spelling correction with word-frequency autocomplete.

Oryn: launching my own iron supplement brand

Oryn sells one product: a gentle iron supplement for women who are tired in a way sleep doesn't fix. I started with what thousands of these women already say online, and built the product, offer and store around it.

~3,600real customer voices read before building anything
6sources: Reddit, YouTube, TikTok, Amazon reviews, competitor reviews and ads
1product, live and taking orders

What the research found

Before building, I read 404 Reddit threads, 1,776 YouTube comments, 763 TikTok comments and about 150 critical Amazon reviews, then synthesized them into one customer profile. I noted each source's bias next to its findings.

  • Her real competitor is quitting. Drugstore iron upsets her stomach, so she stops. "Take iron for a week, feel awful, quit. Repeat every 6 months."
  • She blames herself. About 60 comments said some version of "I told myself I was lazy." Most had never heard the word ferritin.
  • She no longer trusts the word "gentle". Too many products promised it and still hurt.

How it shaped the product

  • Built for staying on it, not just absorption. I chose iron bisglycinate with vitamin C, a once-a-day tablet, because the job is getting her to month three.
  • Wrote in her words, not lab terms. The store opens with "Feel like yourself again" instead of ferritin numbers. The copy rules ban promising specific lab results or implying she has a condition.
  • Priced for the habit. Iron takes months to work, so the offer is quantity bundles, 2-, 6- and 12-month plans, and a 90-day guarantee.
  • Cut scope instead of slipping. The first version paired iron with beef liver. When no supplier could make both, I relaunched as Oryn Gentle Iron rather than wait.

Fiturnly: cutting returns at the size chart

Over half of apparel returns come down to size and fit. On a resale site it's worse, because every item is a single piece that may have been washed, shrunk, or altered.

$890Byearly cost of U.S. retail returns
20–30%typical clothing return rate
2–5%our target reduction in fit-related returns

What we specified

Existing tools like True Fit and 3DLOOK recommend a size, but they don't learn from what shoppers send back. Fiturnly closes that loop without asking anyone to scan their body.

FeaturePriorityWhat it does

07 / Product strategy
Context
CMU Product Management Academy
Role

Methods
Personas, user research, risk mapping, metric design

LinkedIn Mentorship Hub

Students without family or alumni connections struggle to find mentors who recently walked their path. We designed a Gen Z companion app to LinkedIn that matches them with those mentors.

80M+Gen Z university students, the market it opens
5,000active mentor-mentee matches, MVP goal
60%repeat engagement, expansion goal

North star metric

Mentorship engagement rate: verified, recurring interactions (two or more sessions or active message threads) within 30 days. A signup alone doesn't count, because we wanted a metric that only moves when mentoring actually happens.

The biggest risk

Mentees will outnumber mentors. To get more mentors, we planned certified mentor badges, visibility boosts in feed and search, and mentoring achievements that show on the main LinkedIn profile.

Chefstack and StudyConnect

Two products I took from sketch to high-fidelity mockup: a recipe app for phones and a web app for finding campus study groups. For each one I wrote out the reasoning behind every layout choice.

A detail I'm proud of

Chefstack's recipe cards have no boxes. The title sits 6 px under its photo and 16 px from the next card. That 2.5× spacing ratio is enough for each card to read as one unit.

A navigation choice

In StudyConnect, each group's recent activity lives under that group instead of in a separate feed. Students see what changed where they already look, and the red dot marks unread messages without extra text.

09 / Service design
Client
Sidecar, a local Pittsburgh food business
Deliverable
Strategy presentation: research, blueprint, user flow, metrics, tradeoffs

Sidecar: catering for student clubs

Sidecar wanted more community reach. We compared three growth options and picked catering, because student clubs order once a week or more, more often than any other customer group.

Recommendation

Partner with student organizations through pilot events, and market catering on Instagram. Small website changes help too: larger packages, serving-size info, and a mail merge to reach hundreds of Pittsburgh clubs with personalized messages.

How we'd measure it

Catering orders per week, revenue, average order size, repeat customers and on-time delivery. Post-order questions on quality and reorder intent would feed back into the packages.
